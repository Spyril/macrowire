Request to ASX for written consent under clause 5(b)
====================================================

Date sent:  21 August 2026
To:         ASX
From:       repository owner
Subject:    Request for written consent for automated access to company
            announcements

What was asked, and when
------------------------

On 21 August 2026 the repository owner asked ASX for written consent under
clause 5(b) of the ASX Terms of Use, for automated access to company
announcements on these limits:

  - personal, non-commercial use
  - a watchlist of approximately 20 securities
  - polled a few times a day
  - stored locally
  - never redistributed

Clause 5(b) prohibits automated access "without prior written consent",
which means the prohibition has a documented way through it. Asking is the
only way to find out whether that way is open in practice.

What came back
--------------

Consent was declined. ASX does not allow retail end users to scrape the
ASX website, and directed the request to its published list of data
vendors and ISVs as the only route for sourcing the data. The reply is
recorded in 2026-08-25-asx-reply-ASXO-ASXLEGAL.FID2125721.txt.

Reference
---------

ASX reference:  ASXO-ASXLEGAL.FID2125721
Date received:  25 August 2026
Issuing office: ASX, Office of General Counsel

That reference is what makes this position checkable. Anyone who doubts it
can put it to ASX and get the same answer, which is a stronger thing than
a quotation in a repository - a quotation can only be read, a reference
can be verified against the party that issued it.

Retention
---------

The verbatim correspondence is retained privately by the operator and is
available on request.

It is deliberately not reproduced here. Consent was sought to ACCESS the
site; permission to republish ASX's own words was never asked for and
never given. A project whose whole position is that it asks first should
not publish someone else's letter on the strength of having received it.
